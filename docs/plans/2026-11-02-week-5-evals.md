## Steps

### 1. Read Hamel Husain, *Your AI Product Needs Evals*
https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/

Read it for the reasoning: why teams stall without evals, his three levels (assertions, then human and model review, then A/B tests), and why he says to look at your data before choosing metrics.

Write short answers in your notes:
- Which checks does `/ask` already have at level 1? (Think `CitationCheck`, the distance gate, the repair call.)
- Which of last week's failures can only be caught at level 2? (The likely ones: invented claims, wrong citations, general knowledge instead of abstaining.)

**Answers**

Level 1 isn't the same as the tests in `AiLab.Rag.Tests`:
- **`AiLab.Rag.Tests` checks the code.** The `ScriptedChatClient` writes the model's reply in advance. The 28 `/ask` tests prove the code reacts correctly, for example a made-up `[7]` becomes unverified and a cut-off reply returns a 502. They never check what the real model does.
- **Hamel's level 1 checks the model.** You run the system on real inputs, run quick assertions over the real outputs, and track the pass rate over time. His examples: "exactly one item returned", "no UUIDs in the output". The pass rate doesn't have to be 100%; where you set it is a product decision.

*Level 1 checks `/ask` already has* (the offline tests prove they work; they've never been run on a question set against a real model):
- `CitationCheck`: every cited `[n]` was sent
- the distance gate: unrelated questions stop before the model
- the repair call and the 502 codes: unreadable or cut-off replies are caught
- the typed `AskAnswer` parse: the reply has the right shape

The 30-question set is the input those assertions will run on.

*Failures that only level 2 (a human or a judge model) can catch*, because they pass every assertion:
- **M1, invented:** cites a real source, but the claim isn't in it.
- **M2, wrong citation:** the right fact, cited to the wrong chunk. `CitationCheck` only checks that the number was sent.
- **M3, left out:** for example B7 missing "refresh 30 per minute". Catching this needs a gold answer.
- **M4, general knowledge instead of abstaining:** the reply looks like a normal grounded answer.

These four were most of llama3.2's failures last week. Before trusting a judge, compare its verdicts with your own labels on last week's runs, as Hamel advises.

### 2. Read Eugene Yan, *Task-Specific LLM Evals that Do & Don't Work*
https://eugeneyan.com/writing/evals/

Read it for which metric fits which kind of task. `/ask` is really several tasks: finding the right chunk, deciding whether to answer, getting exact facts right, and staying faithful to its sources. Match each one to a metric, and note which metrics he warns against.

Write down a shortlist of no more than six metrics. You'll use it in step 5.

**Answers**

The article is organised by five **task types**, not metrics: classification, summarization, translation, copyright, toxicity. Each section has its own metrics. For `/ask`:

| Task type | Useful for `/ask`? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Classification / extraction | **Yes** | Deciding whether to answer, abstain or gate is a classification. Exact facts are extraction. |
| Summarization | **Yes, the most useful** | An answer built from chunks is a short summary of them. His "factual consistency" eval is faithfulness. |
| Translation | No | H5 failed at retrieval, not because of a bad translation. `/ask` doesn't produce translated text. |
| Copyright | No | Copying the docs word for word is fine; they're the source. |
| Toxicity | No | Internal API docs, developer users. It matters for a public chatbot. |

*The four jobs matched to metrics:*

| `/ask` job | Closest section | Metric | How it's scored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finding the right chunk | None (search ranking isn't in the article) | recall@5, recall@10, MRR | Is the gold section in the top k? Automatic, no model call |
| Deciding whether to answer | Classification | precision and recall of "should abstain"; separation of distributions; ROC-AUC across `MaxDistance` values | Label each question answerable or not, compare with what `/ask` did |
| Getting exact facts right | Classification / extraction | exact match; precision and recall of list items | `Jwt__Secret` exactly? All 5 of B7's limits? |
| Staying faithful to sources | Summarization (factual consistency) | supported or not, per citation (he uses an NLI model; you'd use an LLM judge) | Source chunk is the premise, answer sentence is the claim: does the source support it? |

*Separation of distributions, applied to the gate:* plot each class's scores and check whether they overlap. You already have the data. Answerable questions had distances of 0.26–0.48, not-answerable ones 0.36–0.45. They overlap completely, so no `MaxDistance` value can be a quality filter. The gate can only catch clearly unrelated questions.

*What he warns against:*
- **Accuracy on its own:** too coarse. It hides whether the errors are answering when you should abstain, or abstaining when you should answer. Report precision and recall separately.
- **Word-overlap and embedding-similarity metrics** (ROUGE, BLEU, METEOR, BERTScore, MoverScore): good and bad answers get similar scores, so no threshold separates them. Week 4's negation pair at 0.94 similarity is the same problem.
- **G-Eval** (an LLM grading on a scale): unreliable, at least twice the tokens, misses subtle errors. Use pass/fail instead.
- **LLM judges without checks:** in the HaluEval results he cites, ChatGPT and Claude 2 were only about 54–59% accurate at telling faithful summaries from hallucinated ones. Check the judge against your labels first.

*Shortlist for step 5:*
1. recall@5
2. MRR
3. precision and recall of abstaining
4. separation of distances, answerable vs not
5. exact match and list completeness
6. faithfulness, pass/fail per citation

Avoiding: ROUGE, BLEU or BERTScore against gold answers, and 1–10 LLM scores.

### 5. Write the eval plan

Organise it in three layers, cheapest first:

**Retrieval (no model, free):** doc hit@5, section recall@5, recall@10 for questions that need two documents, MRR.
Starting thresholds: doc hit@5 ≥ 95% and section recall@5 ≥ 90% on answerable English questions. Report the French question on its own.

**Abstaining:** report precision and recall of "should abstain", not just accuracy. The out-of-domain question stops at the gate. No answerable question gets gated. Haiku says "not in the docs" on ≥ 90% of `abstain` runs. Plot the best distance of answerable vs not-answerable questions (separation of distributions) to show why the gate can't do more.

**Answers (Haiku, 3 runs per question; llama3.2 tracked only):**
- correctness: pass/fail per run; a question passes if 2 of 3 runs pass; threshold ≥ 85% of questions
- exact match on names and numbers: 100%
- complete lists: every item present
- faithfulness: each citation supports its claim; scored by hand this week, by an LLM judge later; threshold ≥ 90%
- format failures: at most 1
- cost and latency: tracked, no threshold yet

**Regression rule:** a change is accepted only if every threshold holds and no question that used to pass now fails.

Say clearly in the plan that the thresholds are **provisional** until the first full baseline run. Also note that with 30 questions, one question is worth 3.3 points.
