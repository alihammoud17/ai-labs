## Takeaways from the readings

### Hamel Husain, *Your AI Product Needs Evals* (https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals/)

- **Tests and evals are different.** `AiLab.Rag.Tests` uses a scripted fake model and proves the code reacts correctly: a made-up `[7]` becomes unverified, a cut-off reply returns a 502. An eval runs the real model and measures its answers.
- **Look at the data before choosing metrics.** Week 4's failure log did this: reading every run showed that `/ask` fails at generation, not retrieval (llama3.2: 21 generation codes, 1 retrieval).
- **Pass/fail, not grades.** A simple "right or wrong, and why" is easier to check and act on than a 1–10 score.
- **Some failures pass every automatic check.** `CitationCheck`, the gate and the repair call can't see an invented claim with a real citation (M1), a fact cited to the wrong chunk (M2), a left-out item (M3), or general knowledge instead of "not in the docs" (M4). Those need a human or a judge model.

### Eugene Yan, *Task-Specific LLM Evals that Do & Don't Work* (https://eugeneyan.com/writing/evals/)

- **Pick the metric per task.** `/ask` is several tasks: finding the chunk (recall@5, MRR), deciding whether to answer (precision and recall of abstaining), exact facts (exact match, every list item), and staying faithful (does the cited chunk support the claim).
- **Avoid ROUGE, BLEU and BERTScore.** They measure word or meaning overlap, and good and bad answers score too close together. Week 4's negation pair at 0.94 similarity is the same problem.
- **Check any LLM judge against human labels.** In the results he cites, judges told faithful from invented summaries only 54–59% of the time. Before trusting a judge on `/ask`, compare it with the week 4 hand labels.
